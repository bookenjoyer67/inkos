# Chapter 5: The Cheddar Bulldozer

The bulldozer arrived at nine on Friday morning, yellow paint gleaming, blade lowered like a jaw. It parked at the edge of the loading dock, and a crew in Hargrove Development vests started unloading barriers. The demolition was supposed to be Sunday. The machine was early. The point was the machine itself — sitting there, patient, bigger than any table.

Maren watched it through the stall window. The bread in her hands was still warm from the oven. She had been planning to serve it at lunch.

"I can't stop it," she said.

"Correct," the Rind said from the counter. "You could, however, block it."

"Grandma used to say a table never stopped a bulldozer."

"Your grandmother never built a table from cheddar."

Maren's laugh came out ragged. She tore the bread, pressed a wedge of the Rind between the halves, and ate it standing up, watching the machine through the glass. The cheese melted on her tongue like a dare.

"I wish I could block the bulldozer."

The Rind shivered its whole rind and said, "Done."

The scraping noise from the loading dock was enormous — a sound like six tons of cream being poured into a mold. The vendors streamed out of their stalls. The crew stopped stacking barriers. Maren stepped outside and stared.

A second bulldozer — or the shape of one — now sat across the loading dock between the real machine and the market. But this one was cheddar. Bright, absurd, magnificent cheddar. The wheels were wheels of aged gouda. The blade was a slab of something so sharp-smelling the air fizzed. A sign in the cab read: PROGRESS IS A GRILLED CHEESE.

It was six feet tall and forty feet long and completely immovable. The real bulldozer couldn't get past it. The loading dock was sealed.

"That's cheese," one of the crew said.

"That's a cheese bulldozer," Priya corrected.

Maren's phone started buzzing. Then Priya's. Then Omar's. The cheddar bulldozer was everywhere. Someone had filmed it from the roof across the street. Forty thousand views in an hour.

"Viral," Nico said, coming up beside her. "That's some — is that cheese waving?"

The cheddar bulldozer was not waving. The noon sun had begun to soften it. A small amber river, fragrant and butter-bright, trickled out from under the wheels.

"It's melting," Maren whispered.

"It's forty feet of cheese," Nico said. "In the sun."

The stream spread. The fragrance deepened. Pigeons arrived. Then a health inspector in a mask, carrying a clipboard like a shield.

"Who is responsible for this?"

Maren stepped forward. Her phone was still buzzing.

"I need this structure removed," the inspector said. "The city's position is that this constitutes a public health hazard. The drainage situation alone — the loading dock can't flood with cheese. It compromises emergency access."

"It's blocking the bulldozer."

"Then it's blocking everything. You have forty-eight hours to remove it, or the city impounds the block." The inspector's professional composure cracked, briefly. "I want to be clear: this is the most absurd thing I have seen in fifteen years, and I have seen a rat eat a muffin out of a vending machine. Please tell me you have a plan."

Maren looked at the melting structure, magnificent and drooling, its amber river curling toward the market's back door.

"Three wedges left," the Rind said softly. "Two wishes, and the one that's waiting."

The cheddar bulldozer groaned, shifted, and shed another warm river of cheese across the stones. Maren watched it come.
